Overview

Customers wanted to refer their friends, and a tool for it already existed, but it sat where they never looked. This is how I found that signal, chose it over other bets, scoped a first release, and set up measurement that could tell real lift from noise.

Skills shown: data-driven discovery, AI-assisted research, prioritization, lean scoping, risk management, outcome measurement.

1. Finding the Signal

I used two sources. An AI pipeline I built reads customer support conversations to show what people are doing by hand. Behavior data showed customers improvising their own sharing workarounds, with spikes around every promotion.

Together they said the demand was real and the problem was discoverability, not a missing feature.

2. Choosing the Bet

I compared it with other candidate initiatives on two questions:

  • How directly would it move the metrics that matter: new customers, retention and order size?
  • What did engineering feasibility reviews say about cost and ROI?
It won on both.

3. Scoping v1

I cut the release to the smallest version that could produce a clean read, and deferred variations and integration with legacy tools. It launched where customers already shop instead of where the old tool lived.

4. Managing Risk

I named the risks before launch:

  • Pulling activity away from an existing channel
  • People gaming incentives
Guardrails and reporting by customer segment let us watch both. The feared cannibalization turned out to be activation of a segment that had gone quiet.

5. Measuring the Outcome

I defined success metrics before launch and scoped a dashboard to track them. Reading the data daily separated the launch spike from steady state and tied later spikes to incentive changes.

I've held off on retention and cost-per-customer claims, because overlapping promotions make them too noisy to call yet.

Takeaway

When customers build workarounds, the demand already exists. Fix discovery before building something new.
